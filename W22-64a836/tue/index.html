What 'days on market' actually tells you — and when it misleads you

Script · Educational explainer
Explainer ~75 sec on camera

Hook

Suggested cold-open — adopt or rework in your voice.

"Realtors cite days on market like it's the whole picture. It's one signal.
Here's what it's actually telling you — and when it sends you the wrong way."

Beats

Structure + intent per beat. Deliver in your own words — these aren't lines to read verbatim.

  1. 0-8s Beat 1
    Open with the misconception: buyers use DOM as a proxy for demand, assuming high DOM = unwanted property. That's too simple.
    Camera Tight headshot, direct eye contact.
  2. 8-22s Beat 2
    What DOM actually measures: the number of days between first listing and pending contract. In Fort Lauderdale right now, the average across 9 priority ZIPs is 89–143 days. That's not a slow market — that's the market. Buyers touring in a 100-day DOM environment have leverage they didn't have two years ago.
    Camera Headshot. Could hold a printed ZIP comparison sheet (illegible) as a prop.
    B-roll 3-sec cut to a desk printout of ZIP data, camera pulling back to reveal the whole spread.
  3. 22-42s Beat 3
    When high DOM is actually useful: a listing at 138 days (33304 average right now) has been through at least one price-reduction cycle. The seller knows what the market will pay. That's useful clarity. You're not fighting a delusional ask — you're negotiating against a number someone has already adjusted.
    Camera Back to headshot. Conversational.
  4. 42-58s Beat 4
    When DOM misleads you: thin-inventory ZIPs. 33306 (Coral Ridge waterfront): 71 active units. DOM data is sparse. When there are only a handful of transactions, DOM averages swing on one or two outliers. Use price-cut rate (27.4% in 33306) and pending count (5) to supplement — DOM alone is too thin.
    Camera Headshot. Consider holding up fingers for the specific numbers.
  5. 58-75s Beat 5
    The takeaway: DOM tells you about the negotiating environment, not the property. Before you skip a listing because it's been up 120 days — find out why. That's usually where the conversation starts.
    Camera Direct. Warm. No rush.
